Then vs. Now

A typical pipeline in 2006 and in 2026

Both columns describe a mid‑sized company's analytics pipeline. Neither is universal; they are the common shape of each period.

2006 nightly batch, on‑prem

  1. Sources: Oracle or DB2 OLTP systems, mainframe extracts, CSV files dropped on an FTP server.
  2. Extract + transform: Informatica PowerCenter, DataStage or SSIS jobs on a dedicated ETL server; transforms happen before load.
  3. Staging: relational staging schema, truncated and reloaded each night.
  4. Warehouse: Kimball star schemas in Teradata, Oracle or SQL Server, sized for peak load years ahead.
  5. Serve: OLAP cubes (Essbase, SSAS) and reports in Business Objects, Cognos or MicroStrategy.

2026 continuous, cloud, governed

  1. Sources: SaaS APIs, Postgres/MySQL change data capture, event streams, documents and logs.
  2. Ingest: managed connectors (Fivetran, Airbyte) and CDC/streams (Debezium, Kafka, Kinesis) land raw data with schema tracking.
  3. Store: Parquet in object storage under an open table format (Iceberg or Delta), registered in a catalog (Unity Catalog, Polaris, Glue) that holds permissions and lineage.
  4. Transform: SQL models in dbt or Spark jobs, bronze→silver→gold layers, tests and data contracts enforced in CI.
  5. Serve: BI and a governed metrics/semantic layer, ML features, vector indexes, and AI agents querying through MCP or platform APIs.

    2026

    Hope → to proof

    2026
